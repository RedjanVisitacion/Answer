What is the Greek term for "happiness" or "flourishing" in Aristotelian ethics?

  • Eudaimonia

According to Aristotle, which type of virtue is developed through teaching and experience?

  • Intellectual Virtues

Which of the following is not one of the Five Basic Relations in Confucian ethics?

  • Teacher – Student

In Buddhist ethics, which concept describes the idea that actions have consequences that shape future experiences?

  • Karma

According to John Rawls, which principle is central to "Justice as Fairness"?

  • Equal access to opportunities regardless of background

How does Aristotle’s Doctrine of the Mean define virtue?

  • Virtue is the mean between two vices: excess and deficiency

What is a common criticism of Aristotelian virtue ethics?

  • It lacks clear and specific rules for action, making it difficult to apply in moral dilemmas.

How does Confucian ethics differ from Aristotelian ethics in defining virtue?

  • Aristotle focuses on individual character, while Confucius emphasizes social harmony and relationships.

Buddhist ethics teaches that suffering arises due to craving and attachment. In a modern consumerist society, where people are constantly encouraged to desire more, how might Buddhist ethics be applied to promote well-being?

  • By encouraging mindful consumption and reducing attachment to material things.

A government is debating how to allocate healthcare resources. One group argues that resources should be given equally to all citizens, while another believes that those who contribute more in taxes should receive better healthcare services. Based on theories of distributive justice, which perspective aligns most closely with capitalist justice?

  • Allocating more healthcare benefits to those who contribute more economically.

Which of the following best describes Kantian Ethics?

  • Ethics based on universal moral principles and duties

According to Kantian ethics, which of the following is a key feature of moral duties?

  • Moral duties must be performed out of respect for the moral law, not for personal gain.

In Kantian Ethics, what is the principle that commands individuals to act in such a way that their actions could be made into a universal law?

  • Universalizability

Kant's Deontological theory asserts that actions are morally right when:

  • They are performed out of duty and in accordance with moral rules

According to Kantian Ethics, why is the spread of fake news considered morally wrong, even if it is legal?

  • It violates the duty of truthfulness and fails universalizability

In Kantian Ethics, why is universalizability important when evaluating actions?

  • It ensures that everyone is treated with equal respect

How does Kantian Ethics view the moral worth of an action?

  • It is determined by whether the action fulfills a moral duty

Which of the following dilemmas aligns with Kantian Ethics’ emphasis on individual autonomy and human dignity?

  • The doctor honoring a patient's decision to refuse treatment, even if it means death

In the debate over digital privacy and national security, which ethical theory would most likely prioritize individual privacy rights over security concerns?

  • Deontology

Which ethical framework would most likely oppose the legal practice of contractualization in the Philippines?

  • Kantian Ethics, due to its emphasis on human dignity and fairness
